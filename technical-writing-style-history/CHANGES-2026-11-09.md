# Changes, 2026-11-09

Two sessions of owner feedback produced new rules whose examples and exemplars reused the nouns of the documents under review. This change keeps the rules, rewrites every example in a different field, and adds a guide so later changes stay general.

## New

- `references/maintaining.md` and a "Changing this skill" section in `SKILL.md`. The guide requires a rule stated without session nouns, a reach test against two unrelated documents, and new nouns in every example. It also bans real identifiers and checker exemptions fitted to one document, allows one exemplar per source, and asks for a dated entry here.
- Register rows for audits and investigations and for sensitive internal critique.
- A Flag for a verbless three-part list standing as a sentence. The existing Fail still covers the One, No, Zero, and Every form, and `tests/triad_flag.md` covers the new Flag.

## Owner decisions applied

- Recommendations read "we should" or "I plan to." The Slack row no longer bans one phrasing while an exemplar used it.
- RCAs name an actor for every action, including in the chronology, and the author owns their own defects in first person. The row drops "Blameless."
- An audit may open with one sentence of the symptom before the finding.
- A plain idiom that carries a judgment the facts support is allowed. The bans on dead metaphors and figurative coinage still apply.
- Time zones: name the zone on every clock time and follow the requester's choice. The rule to always convert to the reader's zone is gone.
- Gate 1's claim ledger and gate 12's link record apply to documents and not to chat replies.
- The technical mapping and replacement evaluation shapes are gone, because the owner rarely writes either. The contract's Mode field now points to a register row, so the skill has one list of document types.

## Overfitting removed

- Five exemplars and about eight inline examples came from one design proposal. Each now uses a different field, and the invented-qualifier exemplar merged with the borrowed-jargon exemplar under vocabulary fidelity.
- One incident appeared in three exemplars. Exemplars 7, 9, and 12 now use invented incidents from different fields.
- The uncertainty exemplar no longer repeats the inline example from the Uncertainty section.
- The exemplar header now lets an AFTER add a fact only where the BEFORE's vagueness stood in for it. Exemplars 1, 2, and 9 already worked this way.
- Checker: removed the `may`/`could` lookahead that exempted two phrases, removed `deliberately` from the finality verbs, added `confirms`, and added general evaluative words to the announcement pattern.
- Fixtures `new_rules.md`, `ban_in_table.md`, and `list_fragments.md` use generic nouns.
- Fixed exemplar 17, which promised four requirements and listed three.

## Source correction and graded defaults

The owner named Bernoff's *Writing Without Bullshit* as the primary source for work documents, with Zinsser's *On Writing Well* and Strunk and White's *The Elements of Style* behind it. Readers checked each book against the skill. A rewrite built on Bernoff's process then lost a blind comparison against both the previous skill and this working tree, so this change keeps the working tree as the base and ports only what the comparison and the books support.

### Sources

- `references/source_rules.md` is rebuilt from the three books with page references, short quotes, and a list of house rules that go beyond the sources.
- Corrected misattributions: the weasel budget of one is a house number (Bernoff's target is a 90 percent cut); the reader's attention is "tens of seconds," not fifteen; "get rid of the Latin" is not Zinsser's wording; Zinsser does not defend the fragment; the fat-word list is ours.
- The paragraph rule follows Strunk and White's Rule 13: the first sentence's subject shows the topic, and the sentence states a fact rather than a label. Zinsser's surprising last line is limited to essays.

### Sentence variety

- New rule from Strunk and White's Rule 18: a run of loose two-clause sentences (`, so`, `, which`, `, and`) reads as singsong. "Do not chop causation" no longer names `so` as its connector.
- The checker flags three loose sentences in a row. The exemplars dropped from 22 uses of `, so` to 11, most of them in deliberately bad BEFORE text.
- Describe the current state once instead of opening each point with "Today."

### Defaults from the owner's grading

Each comes from the owner's picks in the blind comparison and is a default, never a ban.

- A table when three or more items share two or more attributes, a plain list when each item is one sentence, prose for one or two items. Chronologies are tables.
- Plain sentences by default; paragraphs that open with a bolded sentence are not the default structure.
- No formal or deferential asks of the reader. The next step is stated plainly.
- RCAs: first-person ownership without ceremony, and a matter-of-fact summary.
- Slack: two or three short paragraphs, never one block.
- Tickets: the fix is a sentence in the body rather than its own section; neutral voice, with first person allowed for work the writer did.

### Checker

- Exclamation points fail.
- `very`, `quite`, `pretty much`, `sort of`, and `kind of` count as weasels.
- `may` and `could` match only in lowercase, so the month May no longer counts as a weasel.
- A sentence that opens with a pronoun subject ("I plan to...") no longer counts as a long subject-verb gap. The old heuristic pushed writers away from the recommendation wording the skill asks for.
- New fixtures: `loose_run.md`, `exclamation.md`, `month_may.md`, `pronoun_subject.md`.

### Process

- `references/maintaining.md` adds a blind comparison step before adopting any change larger than a wording fix.
- The five comparison prompts are saved in `eval-prompts/`. Every system and fact in them is invented.
